Quick answer: How much quantity should you trade?

Your position quantity should be the smaller of your risk-based quantity and your buying-power-based quantity. This calculator applies both limits so the planned stop-loss risk stays within your selected budget while the position also fits your assumed buying power.

Formula: Risk-based quantity = maximum risk per trade ÷ absolute (entry price − stop-loss price). Buying-power quantity = (capital − safety reserve) × assumed leverage ÷ entry price. Round both down to whole units, then use the lower quantity.

Example: With ₹10,000 capital, ₹1,000 safety reserve, ₹500 maximum risk, 5× assumed leverage, ₹100 entry and ₹95 stop-loss, risk per share is ₹5. Risk-based quantity is 100 shares; buying-power quantity is 450 shares. The calculator therefore returns 100 shares, with ₹500 planned stop-loss risk before charges and slippage.

This is an educational estimate, not a recommendation to buy or sell. Actual margin, execution price and total loss can differ.

Free Position Size Calculator for Intraday Trading in India

Use this free position sizing calculator to estimate how many shares or units you can consider for an intraday setup based on your capital, safety reserve, entry price, stop-loss and maximum risk per trade. It is designed for planning Indian equity-market scenarios, including long and short setups.

The calculator compares a risk-based quantity with a buying-power-based quantity and uses the lower result. It also estimates position value, margin based on your assumed leverage, and potential price targets at 1:2 and 1:3 risk-to-reward ratios.

How to use the position sizing calculator

  1. Enter total trading capital: Add the capital amount you want to use for planning.
  2. Set a safety reserve: Choose an amount in rupees or a percentage of total capital. The reserve cannot exceed total capital.
  3. Set maximum risk per trade: Enter the maximum planned loss in rupees or as a percentage, within the usable capital limit.
  4. Set assumed intraday leverage: Enter a leverage assumption for an indicative buying-power calculation. This is not a live broker margin quote.
  5. Add an instrument and direction: Enter a symbol such as INFY or WIPRO and select Buy / Long or Sell / Short.
  6. Enter entry and stop-loss prices: The stop-loss must be below entry for a long setup and above entry for a short setup.
  7. Review quantity and targets: Check the maximum quantity, planned risk, estimated margin and 1:2 or 1:3 target prices. Export the report to Excel if needed.

Position sizing formula

Risk per share = absolute value of (Entry price − Stop-loss price).

Risk-based quantity = maximum risk per trade ÷ risk per share, rounded down to a whole share.

Buying-power-based quantity = (total capital − safety reserve) × assumed leverage ÷ entry price, rounded down to a whole share.

Maximum quantity = the lower of risk-based quantity and buying-power-based quantity. This helps keep the planned loss within the selected risk budget while also considering the entered buying-power assumption.

How risk-to-reward targets work

The calculator uses the distance between entry and stop-loss as the initial risk per share. For a 1:2 target, the target distance is twice that risk distance. For a 1:3 target, it is three times that distance. Long targets are above entry; short targets are below entry. Gross profit estimates do not deduct brokerage, taxes, other charges or slippage.

Important margin and risk limitations

This is an educational planning tool, not investment advice, a broker margin calculator or an order execution system. Actual intraday margin can vary by broker, instrument, volatility and applicable rules. Stop-loss orders may execute at a different price during gaps or fast markets. Always verify margin and charges with your broker before placing an order. All calculations are estimates and run locally in your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a position size calculator?

A position size calculator estimates the number of shares or units that fit within a selected risk budget and buying-power limit. It uses your entry price, stop-loss, maximum risk and assumed leverage.

How do I calculate share quantity for intraday trading?

Divide your maximum risk per trade by the difference between entry and stop-loss to get risk-based quantity. Then calculate the buying-power-based quantity using usable capital and assumed leverage. The lower whole-share quantity is the maximum suggested by these inputs.

Can I calculate both long and short positions?

Yes. Select Buy / Long when the stop-loss is below entry, or Sell / Short when the stop-loss is above entry. The calculator adjusts the 1:2 and 1:3 target prices to match the selected direction.

Does this show exact Zerodha intraday margin?

No. The leverage field is an assumption. Actual margin requirements vary by broker and instrument and may change. Confirm the required margin in your broker's official tools before placing an order.

Are brokerage and taxes included in the target profit?

No. The displayed target profits are gross estimates before brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST, stamp duty, other applicable charges and slippage.

Does this calculator connect to my trading account?

No. It does not ask for broker credentials, connect to a trading account, fetch live market prices or place orders. Your calculator settings are saved in your browser.

Can I export the position sizing report?

Yes. Use Download Excel to export an Excel workbook containing the account summary and instrument calculations.

Does the calculator guarantee that my loss will not exceed the risk amount?

No. The planned risk is based on the entered stop-loss price. Gaps, slippage, liquidity and execution delays can cause a larger realised loss.
