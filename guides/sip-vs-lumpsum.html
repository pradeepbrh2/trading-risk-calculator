SIP vs Lumpsum: How to Compare the Two

A SIP invests a planned amount at regular intervals. A lumpsum investment deploys a larger amount at one time. Neither method always performs better; outcomes depend on cash availability, market path, horizon and risk capacity.

How SIP investing behaves

Regular contributions spread purchases across dates and change the average purchase cost as markets move. This does not remove market risk or guarantee profit. Each instalment has a different time in the market.

How a lumpsum behaves

A one-time investment puts available money to work immediately. Its outcome depends on market movements after the investment date, including possible declines.

Compare fairly

  • Use the same total contribution budget and time horizon.
  • Test a range of return assumptions, not one optimistic number.
  • Account for cash-flow timing, fees, taxes and withdrawals separately.
  • Consider liquidity needs and ability to tolerate losses.
  • Do not treat projected values as guaranteed outcomes.
