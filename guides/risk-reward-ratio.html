Risk-reward ratio in trading: formula and examples

The risk-reward ratio compares the amount a trade could lose if the stop-loss is reached with the amount it could gain if a target is reached. It is a planning measure, not a prediction of probability.

Formula

Risk = |Entry − Stop-loss|

Reward = |Target − Entry|

Risk-reward ratio = Risk : Reward

Long trade example

Suppose entry is ₹500 and stop-loss is ₹490. Risk per share is ₹10. A 1:2 target is ₹520, and a 1:3 target is ₹530.

Short trade example

Suppose entry is ₹500 and stop-loss is ₹510. Risk per share is ₹10. A 1:2 target is ₹480, and a 1:3 target is ₹470. For a short trade, targets are below entry while the stop is above entry.

Break-even win rate before costs

For a simplified fixed-risk/fixed-reward setup, break-even win rate before costs is Risk ÷ (Risk + Reward). At 1:2, this is about 33.3%. At 1:3, it is 25%. These are mathematical thresholds, not recommended win rates. Costs and losses larger than planned raise the required win rate.

Does a higher ratio mean a better strategy?

Not by itself. A strategy targeting larger rewards may have a lower win rate. Evaluate a meaningful sample of recorded trades, including actual costs and execution, rather than judging from the ratio alone.
