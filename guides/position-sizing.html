How to calculate position size for intraday trading

Position sizing answers a practical question before a trade: how many shares or units can you take while keeping the planned loss within a chosen risk budget? The key inputs are the amount you are willing to risk and the distance between entry and stop-loss.

Formula

Risk per unit = |Entry price − Stop-loss price|

Risk-based quantity = Floor(Maximum risk per trade ÷ Risk per unit)

Floor means round down to a whole unit.

Worked example

Suppose maximum planned risk is ₹1,000, entry is ₹250 and stop-loss is ₹245.

  • Risk per share = ₹250 − ₹245 = ₹5.
  • Risk-based quantity = ₹1,000 ÷ ₹5 = 200 shares.
  • Position value = 200 × ₹250 = ₹50,000.
  • Planned price risk = ₹1,000, before charges and slippage.

Check buying power separately

Risk capacity is not the same as available margin. A basic indicative buying-power estimate is usable capital multiplied by an assumed leverage value. Divide that amount by entry price to estimate a margin-constrained quantity. Actual margin depends on broker, instrument, segment and current rules; an assumed leverage input is not a broker quote.

Use the smaller quantity

A planning calculation can use the lower of risk-based quantity and buying-power-based quantity. This keeps both the stop-loss risk budget and assumed buying-power constraint in view.

Costs and execution matter

Brokerage, exchange charges, statutory levies and slippage can reduce results. Gaps and execution delays can make realised loss larger than planned risk. Verify rates against your current broker tariff and contract notes.
