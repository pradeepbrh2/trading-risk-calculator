Dated cash flows

How XIRR is estimated

Σ [ Cash Flow ÷ (1 + XIRR)(days from first date ÷ 365)] = 0

The calculator solves for the annual rate that makes the net present value of all dated cash flows approximately zero. It uses actual calendar-day gaps and a 365-day year.

Enter at least one negative and one positive cash flow on valid dates. Results may be unavailable for unusual cash-flow patterns with no single meaningful solution. This is an estimate, not a forecast or investment recommendation. Taxes, fees and timing errors affect actual returns.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use XIRR instead of CAGR?

Use XIRR when you invest or withdraw at multiple dates and amounts. CAGR is suitable for a single initial value and a single final value.

Why does XIRR need negative and positive cash flows?

A return calculation needs both invested outflows and received inflows or a final valuation. Without opposite signs, a meaningful investment return cannot be solved.

Can XIRR differ from absolute profit percentage?

Yes. XIRR annualises the timing of cash flows, while absolute return compares total gain with total invested amount without accounting for timing.
